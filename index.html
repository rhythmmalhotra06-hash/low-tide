The tiredness

Sleep isn't fixing it, and you already know that

You get seven hours. You still wake up braced.

By Wednesday your shoulders are somewhere near your ears. By Friday you're too tired to enjoy the weekend you worked all week for.

You've taken holidays. They help for about four days. Then the same tiredness comes back, usually before the laundry is done.

That isn't a discipline problem or a scheduling problem. Your body has run one setting — on — for years, and it has quietly forgotten the way back.

Why this works

Comfort doesn't reset a nervous system. Contrast does.

Most retreats hold you at one gentle setting for three days. Massage, a stretch class, a smoothie. Pleasant, and mostly forgotten by the second week back at work.

Your autonomic nervous system — the part running your heart rate, digestion and stress response — doesn't retrain through comfort. It retrains through range. You take the body up, bring it down, take it up again, with real recovery in between, until it remembers the route.

Three things drive that here.

  • Slow, connected breathingResearch on paced breathing consistently shows a shift toward parasympathetic activity — the rest and digest side of the system. You'll do about six hours of it across the weekend.
  • Deliberate coldTwo to three minutes in cold water produces a sharp noradrenaline response. The practice isn't enduring it. It's staying calm inside it, which is a trainable skill.
  • Genuine recoveryFascia work, sound and long unscheduled afternoons, so the activation has somewhere to land instead of just adding to the load.

What's included

Everything in the three nights

  • Breathwork, twice a day

    You lie down and breathe in a set rhythm for about an hour. Music plays. A guide stays in the room throughout. It sounds too simple to matter, and it shifts how you feel inside the hour.

  • Morning movement

    Fifteen minutes of shaking and stretching. No steps to learn, no mirrors, nobody watching how you look. It warms the body before the cold.

  • Cold plunge, both mornings

    Two to three minutes, supervised, straight after movement. You'll be taught to breathe through it rather than brace against it. Most people feel unusually clear for hours afterwards.

  • Fascia release, twice

    Slow bodywork on the tension you've carried so long you've stopped noticing it. Mostly hips, chest and back. What fascia is, in plain English

  • A sound session

    Forty five minutes lying down while bowls and a gong are played around the room. You do nothing. Most people fall asleep. How it works

  • Cacao, live music and a fire

    Saturday night. No instruction, no performance, no partner exercises. Dance, sit, or watch the fire until it burns down.

  • Unscheduled hours

    Long stretches with nothing planned. Swim, sleep, talk, or nothing. The gaps are part of the design, not a gap in the programme.

  • An optional fast

    Saturday night to Monday breakfast, on water, salts and herbal tea. Roughly half a room takes it. Full meals run all weekend for everyone who doesn't.

In plain English

What fascia actually is

Every muscle in your body sits inside a thin, tough wrapper. Picture the white pith under an orange peel, or the film on a raw chicken breast. That wrapper is fascia.

It runs through the whole body in one continuous piece — shoulders to hips to feet, like a bodysuit worn under the skin.

Sit at a desk for ten years and the bodysuit sets in that shape. It doesn't relax when you stand up.

That's the stiffness stretching never seems to solve. Your muscles aren't short. The wrapper is stuck.

Fascia release is slow, targeted pressure that unsticks it. We work mostly on hips, chest and spine, because that's where desk work and stress collect.

In plain English

What a sound session actually is

You lie on a mat under a blanket with your eyes closed. Someone plays large metal bowls and a gong around the room for about forty five minutes.

There's nothing to do. Nothing to believe in. You don't need a musical ear and you won't be asked to chant.

Most people either fall asleep or drop into a depth of rest they can't reach at home. Nothing to decide. Nothing to reply to.

It runs immediately after Sunday's long breathwork session, when the body has already let go and the sound has somewhere to go.

Straight talk

What I'm not going to claim

Breathwork is not a treatment for depression, anxiety disorders or trauma, and nobody here will suggest otherwise. If you're in care for any of those, this sits alongside that work, not instead of it.

Three nights will not rebuild a life. It interrupts a pattern, which is usually what's needed before anything else can change.

Some people have a large emotional release. Some feel calm and clear. A few feel almost nothing until the following week. All three are normal and I can't tell you in advance which one you'll get.

If anyone promises you a specific outcome from a weekend, be careful with them.

Before you ask

Questions people actually have

I've never done anything like this. Will I be lost?

No. Most of the room will be first timers. Everything is explained in plain language before it starts, and you can stop or sit anything out without explaining yourself to anyone.

Is the cold plunge safe?

For most healthy adults, yes, and it's supervised throughout. You'll be taught to breathe through it rather than brace against it. Tell us in advance if you have a heart or blood pressure condition and we'll adapt it or skip it.

What if I don't feel anything?

That happens and it's fine. Some people cry in their first session, some just feel deeply relaxed, some feel little until day three. There's no correct outcome and nobody is comparing.

Do I have to talk about my feelings in front of strangers?

No. Speaking in circles is optional. Plenty of people listen the whole weekend and get just as much from it.

Do I have to fast?

No. It's offered, not expected, and there's full food for anyone not doing it.

Will anyone touch me?

Only in the fascia sessions, and only if you say yes each time. You can change your mind at any point without a conversation about it.

Can I bring my partner or a colleague?

Yes, and you can share a room. One thing worth knowing: people often go further when they aren't managing how they look to someone who knows them.

Can I do a call or two if work needs me?

Honestly, that's the one rule I'd hold you to. Phones go away Friday night. If you genuinely can't be unreachable this weekend, come to a later one.

What if I need to leave early?

You can. Tell me directly rather than slipping away, so we can close it properly with you.
